A fixed coupon note (FCN) pays a steady coupon as long as a basket of stocks behaves. The catch sits at the **knock-in barrier**: if the worst stock in the basket ever falls below it and finishes below strike, you stop receiving cash and start receiving shares — at a loss. Sellers of these notes love one particular sales exhibit: *"this structure has not delivered shares in years."*

My paper [*When Winning Streaks Mislead*](https://sinclairhuang.org/publications/) asks what that clean streak is actually worth as evidence. This page is the paper's engine made interactive: nine US-listed semiconductor names, every worst-of-3 basket (84 of them), notes started every month from 2000 to 2026 with immediate reinvestment, every note settled by the same code that produced the published numbers. The 60% barrier reproduces the paper's figures to the digit; the other barriers re-run the same engine so you can feel how sharply the cliff moves.

Two things to watch. First, how fast the delivery rate responds to the barrier — the difference between a 50% and a 70% barrier is not a detail, it is the whole product. Second, the last chart: the probability of a delivery in the *next year* stops falling after about four clean quarters. History's calm stretches told you which regime you were in — they did not tell you it would last.
