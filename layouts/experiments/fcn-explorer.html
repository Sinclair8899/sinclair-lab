How to read this page. This is not a product pricer — it is an interactive stress exhibit. Change the knock-in barrier and the horizon, then watch three things:
  1. how often at least one delivery occurs within the reinvestment window;
  2. how deep the bad-tail return becomes;
  3. whether a clean past streak actually reduces next-year delivery risk.
The lesson is not that every FCN is bad. It is that a clean historical streak is a weak safety signal unless the product terms, the underlyings, and the market regime are understood.

Method & limits. This page is an interactive companion to the working paper, not a substitute for the full methodology or a product valuation tool. Results come from the reproducibility package of my working paper When Winning Streaks Mislead (publications): its daily price data, unmodified, and its settlement engine, re-implemented in vectorized form for speed and verified bit for bit against the paper's own code for all 120 configurations offered here (60 term combinations × two horizons). Notes are worst-of-3 baskets over nine US-listed semiconductor names. The working paper's configuration is a 63-trading-day tenor, autocall from day 21, strike 80%, knock-in barrier 60%, and a fixed 12% annual coupon as a comparability convention; windows start monthly with immediate same-basket reinvestment, 2000–2026. "Delivery" on this page is the paper's Scenario-D assignment: no autocall, barrier touched, and the worst name below strike at final observation.

Window metrics aggregate sequential notes over the selected horizon; they should not be read as single-note assignment probabilities. The single-note tile reports that per-note rate separately, pooling every note started every five trading days in every basket, as the paper does (8.1% at the paper's configuration). The paper's configuration reproduces its reported numbers to the digit; every other barrier, strike, tenor and autocall start re-runs the same engine and is an interactive extension beyond the paper. The coupon stays at 12% for every combination — real notes would quote different coupons for different terms — so compare risk across settings, and do not read the returns as offers.

The streak chart re-runs the paper's streak diagnostic for the selected barrier, with every other term at the paper's configuration. Its bands resample whole calendar years of note starts, the paper's block-bootstrap scheme; with 25 year blocks they are wide. Only in the paper's configuration (60% barrier) is the flat tail after about four clean quarters read as a robust pattern; at the other barriers the chart reports point estimates and bands without a claim about the tail. At every barrier, the earlier decline is reported as point estimates whose bands overlap. Overlapping windows are not independent observations, and the nine names are survivors, which flatters the results: because the universe is survivor-only, read these figures as a lower-bound stress exhibit rather than a full market-history estimate, and expect a delisting-inclusive database to deepen the left tail. The page is designed to show the sensitivity of streak narratives to product terms, not to estimate a fair coupon — it prices no product, models no issuer terms, and is not investment advice.
