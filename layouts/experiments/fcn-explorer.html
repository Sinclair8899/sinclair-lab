How to read this page. This is not a product pricer — it is an interactive stress exhibit. Change the knock-in barrier and the horizon, then watch three things:
  1. how often at least one delivery occurs within the reinvestment window;
  2. how deep the bad-tail return becomes;
  3. whether a clean past streak actually reduces next-year delivery risk.
The lesson is not that every FCN is bad. It is that a clean historical streak is a weak safety signal unless the product terms, the underlyings, and the market regime are understood.

Method & limits. This page is an interactive companion to the working paper, not a substitute for the full methodology or a product valuation tool. Engine and daily price data come unmodified from the reproducibility package of my working paper When Winning Streaks Mislead (publications). Notes are worst-of-3 baskets over nine US-listed semiconductor names, 63-trading-day tenor, autocall from day 21, strike 80%, fixed 12% annual coupon as a comparability convention; windows start monthly with immediate same-basket reinvestment, 2000–2026. "Delivery" on this page is the paper's Scenario-D assignment: no autocall, barrier touched, and the worst name below strike at final observation. Window metrics aggregate sequential 3-month notes over the selected horizon; they should not be read as single-note assignment probabilities. The 60% barrier is the working paper's reported configuration — its numbers here reproduce that configuration to the digit; other barriers re-run the same engine and are an interactive extension beyond the paper. Overlapping windows are not independent observations, and the nine names are survivors, which flatters the results: because the universe is survivor-only, read these figures as a lower-bound stress exhibit rather than a full market-history estimate, and expect a delisting-inclusive database to deepen the left tail. The page is designed to show the sensitivity of streak narratives to product terms, not to estimate a fair coupon — it prices no product, models no issuer terms, and is not investment advice.
